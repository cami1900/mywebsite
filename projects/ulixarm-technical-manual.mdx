# UlixArm Technical Manual & CE Conformity

**Scope:** risk assessment, CE conformity, technical writing · **Where:** Itaca Robotics, a robotics startup · **When:** 2026

## What it is
The **UlixArm Research Kit** is a 6-axis robot arm for research labs, sold with a gripper, a depth camera and an electrical panel. Before it could be sold in the EU it needed a risk assessment, a conformity route under the **Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC**, and an instruction manual. I did all three, and the result is an 88-page technical manual (*original instructions*).

## What I did
- **Risk assessment (EN ISO 12100):** worked through every life phase, from transport and installation to operation, maintenance and decommissioning. I identified the mechanical, electrical and thermal hazards, using EN ISO 10218-1 Annex A as a checklist, and defined a protective measure for each one.
- **Conformity route:** classified the kit as **partly completed machinery** and wrote the **Declaration of Incorporation** (Annex II 1.B). It lists the essential requirements the arm already meets and the ones the integrator of the final machine must cover.
- **Safety concept:** documented the stop category 0 emergency stop with its self-latching restart, the mechanical end stops, and the software checks in the robot's API, and was clear about what each of them *cannot* do.
- **Technical manual:** wrote the full manual: safety chapter with graded warnings (DANGER, WARNING, CAUTION), personnel qualifications, specifications, transport, installation, operation, maintenance and an appendix of component datasheets.

## The interesting part: an arm with no brakes
For research, the joints have to stay **backdrivable** (quasi-direct-drive motors), so the arm has no brakes. As a result, cutting the power, even with the emergency stop, lets the arm **fall under its own weight**. That can't be designed out without changing what the product is for, so it became a documented **residual risk**. It is reduced by a payload limit, a 90 °C temperature cut-off, a 0.2 rad command-step limit, and a required protective enclosure around the 840 mm workspace, and each item is assigned either to the manufacturer or to the integrator.
